Drivers and safety

The safety net is the part you're really paying for

安全保障，才是您真正需要的。

For a parent who lives alone, the person at the door matters as much as the food. Here is exactly who that person is, and exactly what they do if something seems wrong.

01 / Our drivers

Chosen for patience, not speed

Our first drivers are people we know: family members and trusted adults. As we grow, we hire retirees and students who want a few steady hours, ideally speaking Cantonese or Mandarin. Interested in driving? They're paid hourly, not per drop, so nobody is rushing your parent at the door.

We only use outside courier services as an emergency backup, for example if a driver is sick. If that ever happens, we tell you in advance.

Every driver

  • Passes a police vulnerable sector check before their first shift
  • Has a clean driving record and car insurance that covers delivery
  • Completes basic dementia-awareness training
  • Is trained on our no-answer protocol and carries a printed copy
  • Keeps the same route, so your parent sees the same face

And never

  • Goes inside your parent's home
  • Accepts money, tips or gifts
  • Leaves food outside unattended

02 / The no-answer protocol

If your parent doesn't answer the door

Every driver follows these steps, in this order, every time.

  1. Knock or ring, then wait

    The driver waits 2 to 3 minutes. Older people can take a while to reach the door.

  2. Call your parent

    From the door, using the number you gave us.

  3. Call you

    Then your backup contact, or the building concierge if there is one.

  4. Call 911 if there are signs of distress

    For example, calls for help from inside, mail or newspapers piling up, or nobody reachable when your parent was expected to be home.

  5. Take the meal back and tell you

    The driver writes down what happened and we message you. There's no charge for that meal.

Before you sign up

We send you this protocol in writing, answer your questions by message, and ask for:

  • Your number, and a backup contact
  • Your parent's phone number
  • Building entry code or concierge
  • Days they're usually out
  • Anything else the driver should know

Going out or staying with family? Just tell us, and the driver won't worry.

03 / If they seem unwell

When something isn't right at the door

Drivers carry a short guide for when a senior seems confused, unwell or upset. They stay calm, stay at the door, and call you. If it looks like an emergency, they call 911 first.

Drivers aren't nurses or personal support workers, and we won't pretend otherwise. What they can do is notice, and make sure you know.

04 / Food safety

Hot when it leaves, hot when it arrives

  • Meals travel in insulated hot bags, kept at 60°C or hotter, as Ontario public health requires
  • We check the temperature at pickup and at the last stop, and keep a log
  • No route takes longer than 75 minutes from pickup to the last door
  • Containers are microwave-safe with lids that are easy for older hands
  • Allergens are printed on every label, and the restaurant confirms every order
